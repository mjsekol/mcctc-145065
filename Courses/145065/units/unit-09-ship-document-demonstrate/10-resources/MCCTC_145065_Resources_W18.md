## 1. Primary reading

**"Thinking Aloud: The #1 Usability Tool,"** Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group ·
`https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thinking-aloud-the-1-usability-tool/` · **Opened.**

**What it is.** A short article on watching people use a system while they say what they are thinking:
cheap, robust, and dependent on a facilitator who mostly listens.

**Why this one.** It names the same limits Monday's rules guard against: a facilitator who prompts can
bias what the person does. Read it before Monday or after, not during a session.

**One difference to name.** The article asks participants to think aloud. The course's operator test
records what people do and say without asking them to narrate. Ask the class which gives better evidence
for an operator panel, and why.

**Time.** 15 minutes. **Level.** On-level.

## 3. Writing Tuesday's entry

**Keep a Changelog, version 1.1.0** · `https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/` · **Opened.**

Read only the list of change types. Tuesday's entry is almost always "Changed" or "Fixed." The course
template splits the entry into "For the operator" and "Underneath" instead, because the shift lead reads
the first part and the next developer reads the second.

**Time.** 5 minutes. **Level.** On-level.

## 4. Why the rollback folder works

**.NET application publishing overview** · `https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/core/deploying/` ·
**Opened.**

**Why this one.** Its framework-dependent section explains why two release folders can sit side by side
on one PC and both run on the same installed runtime, which is why rollback is "start the other folder."

**Time.** 15 minutes. **Level.** Extension.

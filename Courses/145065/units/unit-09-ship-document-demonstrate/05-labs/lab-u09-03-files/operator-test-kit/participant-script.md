# Participant script · Line 3 operator test

**The one page you take into a session. Print it. Keep it in front of you.**
Riverside Fabrication and its Line 3 are a composite, an invented shop.

---

## 1. Before the person arrives

- [ ] Your panel is **already running** in full screen, showing CONNECTED. Set up before, never while
      they watch.
- [ ] Your control partner is behind the divider with `control-script.md` and the simulator.
- [ ] The **printed** user guide is on the desk, face up. Nothing else is on the desk.
- [ ] A printed `session-record-sheet.md` and a pen.
- [ ] Nothing records. No phone on the desk, no screen capture running.
- [ ] You can see a clock without picking anything up.
- [ ] **No hardware.** The panel reads the simulator on this PC. Nobody touches a Pi or a sensor today.

---

## 2. The consent line, word for word

**Read it exactly as written.** People who paraphrase it lose the recording sentence and the stopping
sentence, and those two matter most.

> I am testing a screen I built, and I am not testing you. Anything that goes wrong is information I
> need. I am going to ask you to do a few things and then stay quiet while you do them, and that is on
> purpose rather than rude. I am not recording anything and I am not writing down your name. You can
> stop at any point, for any reason, and you do not have to tell me why. Is that okay?

**Wait for a yes out loud. A nod is not a yes.** If they hesitate, thank them and stop.

Then say:

> This screen is for someone working on a factory line. The paper on the desk is its guide. Use the
> guide as much as you like. If you get stuck, stay stuck for a bit. That is the part I need to see.

---

## 3. The tasks

Read each card aloud from `task-cards.md`, then hand it over. Give your partner the agreed signal and
the task number. Start the clock.

---

## 4. The four things you may not say

```
1  the name of a button      "press ACKNOWLEDGE", "the badge up top"
2  "you can"                 "you can look at the guide"
3  "did that work?"          it tells them something was supposed to work
4  the answer                including with your face
```

**If they ask you a question,** say: "What would you do if I were not here?" Then stay quiet.

**If they are stuck for three minutes,** say: "Let's move on." Record the task as not done. That is a
finding, not a failure.

---

## 5. The closing question

> If you had to do that again tomorrow, what would you do differently?

Write the answer word for word. Thank them. Walk them out.

---

## 6. After they leave, before the next person

- [ ] Your partner switches the simulator back to `normal`.
- [ ] Put a fresh, unmarked guide on the desk.
- [ ] Number the lines on your record sheet.
- [ ] Check the sheet: **no name anywhere.** Write the person's role only, such as "a welding program
      student" or "an office staff member".
